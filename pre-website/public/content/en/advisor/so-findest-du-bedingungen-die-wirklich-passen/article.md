# Part-Time Job During a Pedagogy Degree: How to Find Conditions That Really Fit

Studying pedagogy is a full-time project. Between lectures on developmental theories, writing term papers, and demanding internships, there is often little time to catch a breath. Nevertheless, for most students, a part-time job during their studies is simply indispensable to finance rent, literature, and living expenses.

A classic student part-time job often consists of shifts in gastronomy or retail. But prospective pedagogues, in particular, have the excellent opportunity to link earning money directly with professional development. In this article, you will learn how to find the ideal job during your studies, which legal limits you have to observe, and how to get the most out of your salary at the end of the month.

## Summary

A job alongside a pedagogy degree is more than just a pure source of money. With the right choice of activity, you gather experiences that let you grow personally and professionally. If you know the rules of the game regarding working hours, earnings limits, and insurance, you protect yourself from nasty financial surprises. Check your contracts carefully, use your rights such as paid vacation, and lose your fear of the tax return. This way, you create the perfect balance between the lecture hall and the working world.

## Combining Theory and Practice: The Subject-Related Part-Time Job

Those who specifically look for a part-time job in the field of pedagogy secure a double advantage: The bank account fills up and at the same time you acquire skills that are crucial in your later professional life. Whether as a school companion, in afternoon care, youth welfare, or adult education, you get to know how to deal with different target groups in practice. Later, you can highlight exactly this valuable practical experience on your resume and thus stand out from competitors.

When looking for suitable employment, many face the decision: Student assistant (Hiwi) vs. Working student?

- The Hiwi Position (Student Assistant): If you work directly at your university, you benefit from maximum flexibility. Working hours can usually be scheduled perfectly around your timetable. In addition, you make important contacts with lecturers, which can be very helpful for later final theses.

- The Working Student Job: An activity in an external company or with a social provider, on the other hand, offers you deeper insights into the private sector and real professional life. Often, the hourly wage is set somewhat higher here.

## Working Hours and Studies: How Much is Actually Allowed?

The most important thing first: From a legal perspective, your studies always have priority. To enjoy student benefits with insurance, the maximum 20 hours per week rule applies. If you work more than these 20 hours during the lecture period, you lose your student status in social security and are treated like a regular employee, which results in high deductions.

However, there is an exception: The lecture-free period. During the semester break, students are allowed to work indefinitely. This is where short-term employment during the lecture-free period is ideal. If you work full-time during the semester break: as short-term employment free of social insurance contributions for a maximum of 70 working days or 3 months in the calendar year, you retain your privileged student status as long as you do not work more than 20 hours per week for more than 26 weeks a year in total.

## Finances, BAföG and Insurance: What You Need to Know

The most common question students ask is: How much can a student earn without deductions? The answer depends heavily on your individual situation, especially on whether you receive state support.

- BAföG Receipt: If you receive BAföG, the BAföG earnings limit is your most important benchmark. In 2026, this is an average of 603 euros per month, which corresponds exactly to the mini-job limit. If you earn more over a longer period of time, your BAföG will be reduced accordingly.

- Health Insurance: Another critical point is the family insurance income limit. If you are still legally health-insured free of charge via your parents, your regular monthly income must also not exceed the limit of 603 euros (as of 2026). If you are above this, you must take out student health insurance yourself (which currently costs around 150 euros a month for health and long-term care insurance).

- The Working Student Privilege: If you earn more than 603 euros and do not work more than 20 hours per week, the working student privilege applies. This means: You are exempt from insurance in health, long-term care, and unemployment insurance. The general social security obligation during your studies is thus greatly mitigated for you.

- Pension Insurance: However, there is an exception. There is basically a compulsory pension insurance for enrolled students. In a working student job, you always pay into the pension fund (9.3% of your gross wage). With a mini-job, you can apply for an exemption from the compulsory pension insurance, but it is often sensible to pay the small personal contribution to collect valuable pension months early on.

## Contracts, Rights, and the Right Salary

Before you sign your commencement of employment, you should carefully check your employment contract. Important working student contract contents and notice periods include not only the exact hourly wage and working hours but also the area of responsibility. Ensure that the notice periods are fair; the statutory four weeks to the 15th or to the end of a calendar month often apply here, unless a probationary period was agreed upon.

Many students mistakenly believe they have no rights in a mini-job. The opposite is true! A statutory holiday entitlement always exists for marginal employment. It is calculated not according to the hours worked, but according to the working days per week. For example, if you work two days a week, you are legally entitled to at least eight days of vacation a year (with a 4-day week it would be 16). You are also entitled to continued payment of wages in the event of illness.

As far as pay is concerned, the working student salary varies considerably depending on the industry. While top wages are often paid in IT or engineering, salaries in the pedagogical or social sector mostly range between the statutory minimum wage and about 15 to 18 euros per hour. Nevertheless: Social providers also have a shortage of skilled workers. If you are already in your Master's degree or bring special prior experience, you should definitely negotiate your wage confidently.

## Taxes During Studies: No Reason to Panic

When the first salary flows, the topic of taxes arises sooner or later. As soon as you start your job, you must provide your employer with your tax identification number. At the beginning of the following year, you will receive an important document that you should check carefully. If you want to understand your income tax certificate: It lists in detail your gross annual salary as well as any wage and church taxes paid.

Generally speaking: The basic tax-free allowance for the year 2026 is 12,348 euros. If you earn less in the year, no taxes are due anyway. If you still paid income tax (which can happen with fluctuating income or in tax class VI), you should definitely take action. With a good tax return guide for students, which you can easily find on the internet, you can get overpaid taxes back from the tax office.

In addition, you can deduct specialist literature for your pedagogy degree, travel costs to the university, laptop, and tuition fees as income-related expenses or special expenses. If you make losses in a year (more expenses than income), you can save this as a so-called "loss carryforward" for your first professional years after your studies, saving you cash later when starting your career.
